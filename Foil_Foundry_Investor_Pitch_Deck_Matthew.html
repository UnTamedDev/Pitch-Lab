A clean structure for Matthew

Two decisions, separated by evidence.

Decision one · now

$1,500 operating pilot x 2

Two $1,500 tranches. Six percent of qualifying Foil Foundry and Luminant receipts beginning in month four, until a 1.25× cap is reached. Target: 4-6 months. No equity or claim on unrelated IP.

Decision two · after proof

$8,000 technology round

Separate instrument activated only after the 4–6 month proof gates. The technology round does not silently expand the first investment's scope.

Preferred instrumentTechnology Revenue Participation Note

DurationUp to 36 months
Repayment cap1.50× · $12,000 on $8,000
Defined receiptsSubscriptions, add-ons, onboarding, consumables—less tax, refunds, chargebacks, payment fees
OwnershipNo permanent equity; no ownership of pre-existing IP

Why revenue participation is preferred

A 5–10% profit share sounds simple, but profit can disappear through legitimate reinvestment and accounting choices. Adjusted receipts are easier for both parties to verify. At 7.5%, reaching the $12,000 cap within 36 months requires roughly 47 average $99-equivalent active accounts, before add-ons.

Profit-share alternative

If Matthew prefers profit participation, an illustrative alternative is 10% of clearly defined Technology Operating Profit for 36 months. Because that structure may return less than principal, it requires explicit treatment of unrecovered capital at maturity and is not the recommended first draft.

Approve the operating pilot now. Revisit the technology round only when retention, gross margin, customer savings, and acquisition economics are visible.
